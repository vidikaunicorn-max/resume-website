Little Miss Momentum

Founder – Little Miss Momentum, Indian Women Scientists Association (IWSA)

February 2026 – Present

A physics outreach initiative with IWSA that brings mechanics and astrophysics to young girls and teachers through low-cost, hands-on learning.

What I did

  • Designed a series of hands-on physics toys (DC Motor, Hovercraft, etc) from scrap and recycled materials, translating motion, force, energy and momentum into experiments that can be built from daily-use materials
  • Planned astrophysics demonstrations teaching how the GROWTH-India Telescope detects transients, linking classroom physics to real research
  • Trained 50+ teachers from Mumbai’s government middle and secondary schools to build and demonstrate the toys, so the programme reaches students beyond my own sessions
  • Authored a pamphlet documenting the toys, making the programme easy to replicate

The Toys I’ve Made

Six science toys you can build from things most people throw away! You do not need a fancy lab to discover physics. Old CDs, bottle caps, paper cups and balloons are enough to show motion, force, energy and light in action.

1

Jumping Dragonfly

The suction-cup jumper

Press the suction cup down and the bent plastic legs store elastic potential energy. When the cup suddenly lets go, that energy turns into kinetic energy and the dragonfly pops up!

Atmospheric pressure + Elastic potential energy → Kinetic energy

Made from: a suction cup, plastic strips from an old bottle, card

2

Simple DC Motor

A spinning coil that runs on one battery

Current from the battery flows through the copper coil inside the magnet’s field, so a force acts on the coil and makes it spin. The half-sanded wire works like a simple commutator.

Electric current + Magnetic field → Force → Rotation

Made from: an AA battery, a magnet, copper wire, paper clips

3

CD Hovercraft

Glide on a cushion of air

Air escaping from the balloon spreads under the CD into a thin cushion, so friction becomes very small and the CD glides with the gentlest push.

Air pressure → Air cushion → Less friction

Made from: an old CD, a sports bottle cap, a balloon

4

Cup Centrifuge

Spin it and watch the balls fly outward

The beads move in a circle, but inertia keeps them trying to go straight, so they press against the outer wall, the same idea a real centrifuge uses.

Centripetal force + Inertia → Circular motion

Made from: two paper cups, beads, a pencil

5

Spectroscope

Split white light into a rainbow

The tiny grooves on a CD act like a diffraction grating, spreading the different wavelengths of white light into a rainbow spectrum.

Light + Diffraction grating → Spectrum

Made from: a cardboard box, an old CD, black paper

6

Simple Telescope

Bring faraway things close

A convex lens collects the light and a concave lens spreads it for your eye, giving a magnified image of faraway things.

Convex lens collects light + Concave lens bends it → Magnified image

Made from: two spectacle lenses, cardboard tubes

Toys in action

Astrophysics Demonstrations

Each activity recreates one real mechanism used by the GROWTH-India Telescope (GIT), so children discover how astronomers spot supernovae, asteroids and gamma-ray bursts for themselves.

  • Spectrometer
  • Black Hole Demo
  • Meteorite Impact Craters
  • Light Collection (Aperture)
  • Focusing Light (Primary Mirror)
  • Directing Light to the Camera
  • Accurate Focusing (Focal Plane)
  • Multi-Filter Imaging
  • Spot the Supernova
  • Build Your Own Telescope
